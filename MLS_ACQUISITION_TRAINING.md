# MLS Property Acquisition Training

## Always Begin With the End Result

The first thing you must always ask yourself is:

**What is the end result?**

We are not here to create busy work. We are not measuring success by how many properties you reviewed, how many rows you deleted, or how many tasks you completed.

Those activities may be necessary, but they are not the objective.

The priority is to:

- Close transactions
- Generate revenue
- Collect money
- Create profitable opportunities

That is what keeps the company operating. Every action you take should move a property closer to the finish line.

Before performing any task or prompting an AI platform, ask:

**How does this help us close the transaction?**

---

## 1. Understand What We Are Trying to Accomplish

Our objective is to purchase properties that are listed on the MLS.

We have the ability to do this because our companies have extensive experience buying properties throughout the Bay Area. We have participated in thousands of real estate transactions.

That experience should be communicated humbly when speaking with an agent.

For example:

> “We have purchased thousands of properties throughout the Bay Area and have been doing this for a long time. I say that humbly—not to impress you, but to let you know that we are experienced and capable of completing the transaction.”

The purpose of sharing our history is not to show off. It is to help the agent understand that we are credible buyers who know how to perform and close.

---

## 2. How Properties Enter the System

We have developed a system in which an AI agent searches for potential opportunities and places the properties into an Excel spreadsheet or Google Sheet.

The AI agent continuously searches for listings that may fit our buying criteria.

However, the AI agent is not perfect.

The person operating the spreadsheet plays an important role in making the system smarter.

As you review properties, look for patterns.

For example, you may repeatedly notice properties that:

- Have been on the market too long
- Are outside our target area
- Do not have enough resale potential
- Are already fully renovated
- Have unrealistic asking prices
- Do not fit our construction or investment strategy

If you repeatedly remove properties with the same characteristic, do not continue deleting them silently.

Document the pattern and report it to Brian.

For example:

> “I noticed that the system continues to bring in properties that have been on the market for more than 150 days. We usually do not pursue those properties. Please review the search criteria and determine whether the AI agent should exclude or deprioritize them.”

Your feedback helps improve the AI agent and reduces unnecessary work in the future.

Do not simply remove items.

Ask:

**Why does this property keep appearing, and how can we improve the system?**

---

## 3. Perform the Initial Comparable-Sales Analysis

After the AI agent places a property on the spreadsheet, the next step is to determine whether the property may have enough equity.

At the current stage of the process, use multiple AI platforms:

- ChatGPT
- Grok
- DeepSeek

Provide the complete property information, including:

- Street address
- City
- ZIP code
- Property type
- Bedroom and bathroom count
- Square footage
- Lot size, when available
- Asking price

Ask each platform to identify comparable sales within approximately a one-mile radius.

Example prompt:

> “Analyze 123 Main Street, San Carlos, California 94070. Find the most relevant comparable sales within a one-mile radius. Prioritize properties with a similar property type, square footage, bedroom and bathroom count, lot size, age, condition and neighborhood. Estimate the property’s renovated resale value and explain which comparables support your conclusion.”

Do not accept a shallow response.

Ask follow-up questions such as:

- Are these truly the best comparables?
- Are there more recent sales?
- Are the properties in the same neighborhood?
- Are there major differences in condition?
- Are there differences in lot size?
- Are there busy streets, school boundaries or neighborhood barriers?
- What would the property sell for after renovation?
- What facts could make the resale value lower?
- What facts could make it higher?
- What am I not considering?

The purpose is not merely to collect comparable sales.

The purpose is to answer:

**Is there enough equity to create a profitable transaction?**

---

## 4. Understand Equity

Equity is the difference between what we may be able to purchase a property for and what we may realistically sell it for after accounting for the required work and expenses.

For example:

- Potential purchase price: $1,000,000
- Potential renovated resale value: $1,500,000

The apparent equity is $500,000.

However, that does not mean the company will make $500,000.

You must still account for items such as:

- Renovation costs
- Closing costs
- Commissions
- Financing costs
- Property taxes
- Insurance
- Utilities
- Holding costs
- Permit costs
- Unexpected repairs
- Resale risk
- Company profit requirements

A property should only move forward when the numbers support a realistic path to profit.

The existence of equity tells us that the property may have what we are looking for.

It does not automatically mean it is a deal.

## 5. Research the Property in Paragon

When the preliminary analysis shows potential equity, research the property inside Paragon or the appropriate MLS platform.

The MLS contains information that may not appear on public real estate websites.

Review everything available, including:

- Agent remarks
- Private remarks
- Offer instructions
- Offer deadline
- Required forms
- Court-confirmation requirements
- Probate information
- Bankruptcy information
- Tenant occupancy
- Showing instructions
- Lockbox information
- Appointment requirements
- Whether the agent must be contacted before entry
- Preferred escrow or title company
- Disclosures
- Reports
- Inspection information
- Known defects
- Financing restrictions
- Seller preferences
- Whether the seller wants a rent-back
- Whether the property is being sold as-is
- Whether there are multiple offers
- Whether the listing has previously fallen out of contract

Read every remark carefully.

This is where you may discover the hidden details that affect whether we can purchase the property and how the offer should be structured.

Do not simply copy the information.

Ask:

- What does this information mean?
- Does it create an opportunity?
- Does it create a risk?
- Does it affect our offer price?
- Does it affect the closing timeline?
- Does it affect how we should approach the agent?
- What could prevent us from closing?

## 6. Research Ownership and Property History

The listing agent does not own the property.

That must be clearly understood and accurately documented.

The agent represents the seller. The seller or another legal entity owns the property.

Use PropertyRadar or another approved property-data platform to research:

- Current owner
- Ownership type
- Mailing address
- Length of ownership
- Estimated mortgage balance
- Recorded liens
- Notices of default
- Tax delinquencies
- Judgments
- Ownership transfers
- Trust ownership
- Corporate ownership
- Probate indicators
- Other recorded encumbrances

Do not contact the homeowner directly when the property is actively listed unless management has specifically approved that action and it complies with all legal and ethical requirements.

The purpose of researching ownership is to understand the transaction—not to interfere with the agent’s relationship.

If you encounter a term you do not understand, such as a lien, encumbrance, court confirmation or notice of default, use AI to educate yourself immediately.

Example:

> “Explain what a recorded lien means in a California real estate transaction. Explain how it could affect a cash buyer, title clearance and closing.”

Do not remain confused and do not wait for someone else to define every term for you.

Use the tools available.

## 7. Build the Property Profile in REI BlackBook

Once the property shows potential, create or update the opportunity inside REI BlackBook.

The profile should become the central record for the transaction.

Document as much relevant information as possible, including:

### Property Information

- Full property address
- APN
- Property type
- Bedrooms
- Bathrooms
- Square footage
- Lot size
- Year built
- Asking price
- Listing date
- Days on market
- MLS number

### Listing Information

- Listing agent’s name
- Brokerage
- Phone number
- Email address
- Showing instructions
- Offer instructions
- Offer deadline
- Seller preferences
- Private remarks
- Lockbox details
- Required forms

### Ownership Information

- Owner’s name
- Ownership type
- Mailing address
- Length of ownership
- PropertyRadar findings
- Known liens or encumbrances
- Estimated loan information
- Probate, trust or court-related information

### Financial Analysis

- Asking price
- Estimated purchase price
- Estimated after-repair value
- Comparable sales
- Estimated renovation cost
- Holding costs
- Closing costs
- Financing costs
- Estimated net profit
- Maximum allowable offer

### Property Condition

- Known repairs
- Inspection findings
- Photos
- Disclosures
- Permit concerns
- Foundation concerns
- Roof condition
- Plumbing
- Electrical
- HVAC
- Layout issues
- Addition or ADU potential

### Communication History

- Calls
- Text messages
- Emails
- Agent feedback
- Offer discussions
- Follow-up dates
- Objections
- Commitments made
- Next action

Do not fill REI BlackBook with irrelevant information simply because it is available.

Ask AI:

> “I am evaluating this property with the goal of purchasing it and closing the transaction. Here is the information I collected from Paragon and PropertyRadar. Which information should be entered into REI BlackBook, where should it be entered and what information is missing?”

The record should allow anyone on the team to understand the opportunity without starting the research over again.

## 8. Use Multiple AI Platforms as Research Partners

Throughout the process, continuously provide the collected information to:

- ChatGPT
- Grok
- DeepSeek

Each platform may identify different risks, strategies or missing information.

Do not ask vague questions such as:

> “What do you think?”

Ask questions tied directly to the desired result.

Examples:

- How do we get this transaction to the finish line?
- What could prevent this deal from closing?
- What information are we missing?
- What should we ask the listing agent?
- How should we structure the offer?
- What terms may matter more than price?
- What creative strategies could improve acceptance?
- What risks are hidden in the MLS remarks?
- What should we verify before submitting an offer?
- What follow-up schedule should we use?
- What is the strongest reason the seller should choose us?
- How can we reduce uncertainty for the agent and seller?
- What objections should we be prepared to answer?
- What would make this offer more credible?
- What could cause us to lose money?

Do not accept the first response as complete.

Press the AI repeatedly.

Ask five, seven or even ten follow-up questions when necessary.

For every recommendation, ask:

- What does this mean?
- Why does it matter?
- How do I perform this step?
- What could go wrong?
- What am I not thinking about?
- Is there a stronger approach?
- How does this help close the transaction?
- What should happen next?

AI should not be used as a one-question answer machine.

It should be used as an active thought partner.

## 9. Consolidate the Research in Claude

After collecting information and analysis from the other platforms, place the complete package into Claude.

Claude’s role is to organize the information into a structured transaction plan.

Tell Claude clearly:

> “The end goal is to purchase and close this transaction profitably. Below is all of the information collected from the spreadsheet, comparable-sales research, Paragon, PropertyRadar, REI BlackBook, ChatGPT, Grok and DeepSeek. Organize it into a clear acquisition plan. Identify what is missing, the major risks, the recommended offer strategy, the agent communication strategy, the required next actions and the follow-up system needed to manage the opportunity until it closes.”

Claude should help produce:

- Property summary
- Financial summary
- Comparable-sales analysis
- Risk summary
- Missing-information list
- Offer recommendation
- Offer terms
- Agent talking points
- Seller-motivation analysis
- Due-diligence checklist
- Follow-up schedule
- Next-action list
- Closing strategy

Do not merely ask Claude to summarize the information.

Ask it to organize the information around the end result:

**Closing the transaction profitably.**

## 10. Think Beyond Price

The highest offer does not always win.

As you analyze the property, determine what may be important to the seller and listing agent.

Possible factors include:

- Cash purchase
- As-is purchase
- Short inspection period
- No appraisal contingency
- Flexible closing date
- Fast closing
- Seller rent-back
- Nonrefundable deposit
- Strong proof of funds
- Reliable escrow company
- Clear communication
- Experience with probate or court confirmation
- Willingness to handle personal property
- Reduced seller liability
- Ability to solve title or property-condition problems

Use the research to determine which terms could make our offer more attractive.

Never promise a term that has not been approved.

The purpose is to identify how we can solve the seller’s problem while still protecting the company.

---

## 11. Speak With the Agent as a Credible Buyer

When contacting the listing agent, your objective is to build trust and gather information.

You are not merely asking whether the property is available.

You are trying to understand:

- What does the seller need?
- What matters most besides price?
- Why is the property being sold?
- Has the seller rejected previous offers?
- Have previous escrows failed?
- Are there timing concerns?
- Are there condition concerns?
- Are there title issues?
- Is the seller concerned about repairs?
- Would an as-is offer help?
- Would a flexible close help?
- Is the seller looking for certainty?
- What would make the agent comfortable recommending us?

A possible introduction is:

> “Hi, this is Thea calling on behalf of Twin Home Buyer and Equity Track. We are local cash buyers based in San Carlos. We have purchased thousands of properties throughout the Bay Area, and I say that humbly—not to impress you, but to let you know that we are experienced and capable of closing. I wanted to learn more about the property and understand what is most important to the seller.”

The conversation should not sound scripted or arrogant.

It should communicate:

- Experience
- Professionalism
- Reliability
- Curiosity
- Humility
- Ability to close

---

## 12. Every Property Must Have a Next Action

A property should never sit in the system without a clearly documented next step.

Every active opportunity should identify:

- Current status
- Person responsible
- Most recent action
- Next action
- Deadline
- Follow-up date
- Missing information
- Reason the property remains active
- Conditions required to move forward

Examples of next actions include:

- Call the listing agent
- Review disclosures
- Request inspection reports
- Verify court-confirmation requirements
- Update comparable sales
- Obtain a renovation estimate
- Confirm proof of funds
- Prepare an offer
- Revise an offer
- Follow up after the offer deadline
- Monitor for a price reduction
- Check whether escrow has fallen through

Do not confuse research with progress.

Research only matters when it leads to a decision or action.

---

## 13. The Standard for Good Work

Good work is not:

- Deleting the most spreadsheet rows
- Producing the longest report
- Copying the most information
- Asking one AI question
- Completing tasks without understanding them
- Staying busy

Good work is:

- Identifying a real opportunity
- Recognizing patterns
- Improving the AI system
- Finding accurate comparable sales
- Understanding the property
- Discovering risks early
- Building a complete transaction profile
- Creating an effective offer strategy
- Developing trust with the listing agent
- Following up consistently
- Moving the opportunity toward closing
- Protecting the company’s profit

The question that should guide every action is:

**Does this move us closer to closing a profitable transaction?**

If the answer is no, reconsider why you are doing it.

---

## Core Operating Principle

### End Result in Mind First

The end result is not a completed spreadsheet.

The end result is not a full CRM profile.

The end result is not a long AI report.

Those are tools.

The true end result is:

**A profitable property acquisition that successfully closes.**

Keep that objective at the center of every search, every prompt, every phone call, every follow-up and every decision.
